Diagnosis · current behaviour against the agreed cases

What breaks today, and what should

The same walls twice: what the solver does now, and what the twenty agreed cases say it should do. One defect explains almost all of it — load only ever flows downward, so a brick that loses a seat is modelled as hanging rather than as wedging against its neighbours. Every number here is derived in ARCHING_DESIGN.md and traceable to published masonry figures; none is fitted.

The mechanism

Three drawings that between them account for the cascade, the stepping angle, and the fix.

1 · Two seats, then one — a cliff, not a slope

In running bond a brick rests on two seats whose area-weighted centroid lands exactly on its centre of mass, so the moment is dropped as indeterminate. Remove one and it has one. Three things worsen at once: the area halves, the section modulus falls 4×, and eccentricity goes from exactly zero to 5.625 cm. There is nothing in between — it is a cliff.

2 · Why the front steps at 33.7°

One deletion leaves two half-seated bricks in the course above — the hole has grown half a cell each side. Each course repeats it. The front advances 11.25 cm horizontally per 7.5 cm of course, which is 33.69° to the horizontal, and stops only when the column left above it is under 17.18 brick weights. That angle is the bond pattern, not a coincidence.

3 · What a real wall does instead

The masonry above wedges together and the load walks around the hole to the sides, in compression — 10 MPa against mortar's 0.1 MPa in tension, a factor of 100. The arch pushes sideways, and that thrust is resisted in shear at the springing, which the model already checks. Nothing new is needed in the material data.

The honest tally

Arching fixes every interior deletion exactly and does not fix the outermost one. A brick at a free end overhangs outward with nothing beyond it to push against, so the arch is correctly refused and the ladder starts. That needed a ruling, and it has one.

Ruled 2026-08-06: case 3 stands. The free-end wedge and the staircase corbel are locally indistinguishable, so the mechanism that saves one saves the other — composite vertical action, putting the staircase at 0.369. That makes the two tests currently asserting the staircase falls the things being corrected, and they will be re-derived from the section implemented rather than tuned to suit.

The ruling is internally consistent rather than an arbitrary tiebreak, which is the reason to trust it: case 20 was independently agreed as local loss — the loose toothed bricks at the cut face drop, the mass stands — and composite action produces exactly that, since the wedge holds at 0.369 while teeth with no seat at all still have nothing to hold them. Two judgements made at different times, landing on one mechanism.

The risk it introduces is the opposite one. Unbounded composite depth makes every wall a monolith and removes the last way a structure can fail, so slice 5 ships with a depth limit and a red test proving some wall still comes down because the depth ran out.

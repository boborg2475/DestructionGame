What the wall should do

Twenty configurations with an expected outcome for each, drawn from how real masonry behaves rather than from what the solver currently computes. Five are matched pairs that differ by exactly one variable — cover depth, span, abutment, corbel projection, superimposed load, bond pattern — so a disagreement between the two halves of a pair points at one specific term in the force calculation. Review these, correct any you think are wrong, and the agreed set becomes the integration suite.

What happens to these next

Each agreed case becomes an integration test — gravity on, everything connected, asserting on outcome rather than mechanism, because for a wall a single severed joint is not collapse. The three verdicts map to three different assertions: stands means the piece count above the cut is unchanged and nothing left the structure; local loss means a bounded, named set of pieces fell and the rest did not; collapse means the structure genuinely came down.

The pairs are the valuable part. A solver that gets both halves of a pair right has the term between them modelled; one that gets both halves the same way has no such term at all. Right now the model has no lateral compression path, so I expect it to fail the standing half of every pair while passing the falling half — which is the signature of a model that can only ever say "falls".

Case 18 was drafted as a collapse and revised to stands, because the arithmetic contradicted the intuition. It is the case where the real-world answer turns on workmanship rather than statics — EN 1996-1-1 permits head joints to be treated as unfilled, and in practice they often are, so a wall built badly loses the column while a wall built properly does not. The model has a filled joint with a published cohesion, so it should say stands. If a bond-discriminating pair is wanted later, the version that works is a wide stack-bond removal: a hanging column n bricks across still has only two head joints holding it, so utilisation grows with n while running bond simply arches.

Case 20 is the one I am least certain of and would most like your judgement on. Everything else here I would defend; that one sits close enough to the real limit that it is a genuine question rather than a known answer.
